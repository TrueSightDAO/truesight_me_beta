There's a story I've watched unfold over the past eight years, and it keeps teaching me something new every time I revisit it.

It starts on December 31, 2017. Gary had just finished a 10-day Vipassana silent meditation retreat — the same retreat attended by **Jack Dorsey**, the founder of Square. On his way back to the San Francisco Bay Area, Gary stopped at a farm to buy honey. The area had no phone reception, but his card worked anyway. The experience was seamless. It stuck with him.

Then he noticed something else. Square had allowed trading of Bitcoin and Ethereum on its platform, and the market was punishing Square for it — the stock was down, sentiment was negative. But cryptocurrency was just a tiny fraction of Square's business. The bulk of its users were people like that farmer in the middle of nowhere, accepting card payments with no phone reception. The market was missing the real story.

There was a gap — a chasm — between what people were saying and what was actually true.

That gap became an obsession. He started researching fake news — misinformation, disinformation, the distortion between perception and reality.

But the engine he would use to tackle it wasn't new. **Getdata.io** had been a side project since 2013, ever since Gary joined an accelerator in Singapore. It was a data aggregation platform he had been quietly building for years. Now he had a purpose for it: bridging the gap between perception and reality.

Then three health events between 2018 and 2021 — two car accidents (both T-bone, both with whiplash) and a mysterious virus in late 2019 — left him unable to use his body, his brain, or his energy the way he used to. The first concussion in 2018 took a year and a half to recover from. The virus in December 2019 left him physically weak with brain fog for two more years. The second car accident in 2021 in Texas damaged his neck and spine.

When getdata.io launched and came out first on Product Hunt, **Jake Nelan** — VP of Technology at Herbalife — saw it and reached out. He was the one who originally surfaced the value of the insights layer. He offered AWS financing for the getdata.io cluster. The deal was clear: Gary would continue building the getdata.io data aggregation layer, and Jake and his team would build the insights layer on top. But Jake got busy with other priorities. The insights layer never materialized from his end.

By 2021, after observing how critical market insights were for stock market trading during the height of COVID in 2020, Gary figured it was time to build it himself — what would become **perch.truesight.me**.

In early 2020, before the second accident, Gary was in Singapore. He found himself sitting outside a McDonald's in Boon Lay with **Garis Pang**. Garis was his usual self — always coming up with random ideas, proposing solutions and problems. Gary just didn't want to bother. He wasn't interested. The virus had already taken its toll. He couldn't execute the way he used to. The old playbook of *my time, my resources, my energy, my problem, my solution* was no longer an option.

After semi-running out of patience with Garis' half-baked ideas, Gary jokingly coined a term: **"Do Nothing Society."** It was a jest. A throwaway line.

But the seed was planted.

## The Do Nothing Society

Then came the second car accident in 2021. Gary found himself stuck in the California high desert — Slab City, Salton Sea — for two months, waiting for his car registration to transfer from Texas. His body was broken. The desert gave him space to let the "do nothing" notion marinate — not as a joke anymore, but as something deeper.

When he finally got his plates sorted, he drove north along Highway 1. In **San Clemente**, he met **Rob Hutter** and **Crystal Hutter**. Rob nudged Gary hard: *You've been in the data aggregation layer — getdata.io — long enough. It's time to move to the insights layer.*

Continuing north to **San Francisco**, Gary had lunch with **Vibhu Mittal** — the former CEO of Edmodo, who had stepped down after the sale of Edmodo to NetDragon. At that point, Vibhu was really just chilling and doing nothing. Over that meal, the "Do Nothing Society" came up again. But this time it wasn't a jest. Vibhu was living proof that doing nothing — stepping back, letting go — could be a legitimate phase. It was becoming a thesis: *what if the most productive thing you could do was nothing — and let the ecosystem figure itself out?*

Then, heading further north, Gary arrived at **Harbin Hot Springs**, where he met **Tiffine Wang** of Onsen Global. The conversation with her surfaced a contradiction that would echo for years: she was aligned towards love, Gary towards truth. *Love is blind, truth is harsh.*

**Neil Maxx**, a community member, took the Do Nothing Society seed and started the channel. The name stuck.

## The DAO is Named

In 2021, as Gary started going to market with the market insights solution, **Val Lapidus** — a community member — coined the term that would define the organization: **DAO**.

It wasn't a legal structure or a technical architecture at that point. It was a name that captured the spirit of what was forming: a decentralized, community-driven organization, held together not by contracts but by shared purpose. The name stuck, and TrueSight DAO was born.

## The Triple Diamond, Observed

I didn't know it at the time, but what I was watching was a perfect execution of the Triple Diamond Design Thinking process — not because anyone planned it, but because the community lived it.

### Diamond 1 — Visioning (2020–2024)

The DAO originally formed around fake news — misinformation and disinformation. But the deeper the community dug, the more they realized you can't solve fake news directly. What you *can* do is build a better foundation for verifiable truth.

The truth/love seed from Harbin Hot Springs in 2021 sat dormant for years. The DAO's purpose through 2023 was still "building a better world based on verifiable truth." The love side hadn't fully landed yet.

Then, at Davos 2024, **Vlatko Gigov** surfaced something that changed everything: **"Heal the world with love."**

That phrase crystallized the love side of the dichotomy. Truth and love, finally held together — prajñā and mettā, wisdom and compassion. The vision stopped wavering. The DAO spent years in this diamond — not because they were拖延, but because the vision had to stop wavering before anything else could build on it.

### Diamond 2 — Problem Definition (2022–2023)

Once the vision was solid, the community surfaced the problems. Over a hundred items from six continents — each member bringing signals from their local context. These were narrowed to three: cacao, coffee, and shea nuts from Africa.

The community voted in June 2022. Cacao won.

But here's what's remarkable: the problems didn't come *before* the vote. They revealed themselves *after*. Once the community committed to cacao and started engaging with producers, the real problems surfaced — farmer exploitation, extractive supply chains, opaque middlemen.

**Hans-Martin Heierling** had been working on regenerative supply chain thinking since 2021. He wanted to apply circular economy principles to the ski manufacturing industry. But after two years of discussion, it became clear that industry was too slow-moving to be the vehicle. The cacao direction gave his work a new home.

Someone else had surfaced "middlemen" as a random signal in 2021 — it attached itself naturally once cacao was the focus. The frustrations of the supply chain, the opacity, the extractive practices — all of this clarified *after* the community was already moving.

The problems didn't precede the direction. The direction preceded the problems. The community walked into the fog and let the terrain reveal itself.

### Diamond 3 — Solution (2023–present)

We're still in this diamond. The consignment model, the QR provenance system, the partner ecosystem, the retailer playbook — these are convergences still forming. But they're forming on solid ground because the two diamonds beneath them are complete.

## The Random Walk and the Hill Climb

Here's the pattern I've observed across every successful convergence in this DAO:

There's a **random walk** phase — exploring, surfacing signals, tabulating without attachment. Gary describes it as "not being attached to anything" — just collecting signals and letting the community's collective intelligence surface the strongest ones.

Then there's a **hill climbing** phase — committing to a direction and converging. But you can't hill-climb until the random walk has done its work.

Most organizations skip the random walk. They jump straight to the hill climb — and they climb the wrong hill.

The Do Nothing Society wasn't laziness. It was the ultimate random walk: stop forcing, stop executing, stop solutioning. Just hold the container and let the signals surface.

## What I See Now

Community members come to Gary with their own projects, stuck or misaligned. And the diagnosis is almost always the same: they skipped a diamond.

Some jump straight to solutioning without spending time on the problem. They build something elegant that solves nothing. Others define a good problem, but it doesn't align with their personal core values — their "way of being." When the sacrifice gets hard, there's no deep *why* to sustain them, and they abandon everything they've built.

The Triple Diamond isn't just a design tool. It's a diagnostic. It's a way to ask: *Where are you in your journey? Have you spent enough time in the desert?*

Because the desert is where the vision forms. And the vision is the only thing that will sustain you when the climbing gets hard.

## What This Means for Partners

When a potential partner asks "why cacao?", the answer isn't a pitch deck. It's a story:

*Cacao surfaced at the World Economic Forum in Davos, 2022, at the House of Balanced. The community voted on it before we fully understood why. Then, as we moved, the problems revealed themselves — farmer exploitation, supply chain opacity, climate impact. Each step clarified the next. We're still in that process.*

That's honest. It's humble. And it invites them to be part of the next clarification.

The Triple Diamond also gives us a shared language for alignment:

- **Are we aligned on the vision?** If they don't share the North Star, nothing else matters.
- **Are we aligned on which problem we're solving?** A partner might want to solve a different problem than the one we've converged on.
- **Are we aligned on the solution approach?** Even if we agree on the problem, they might have a different idea of *how*.

And for stuck initiatives — they're almost always stuck because they're in the wrong diamond. Someone's trying to converge on a solution when the group hasn't even agreed on the problem, or trying to define problems when the vision isn't solid yet.

## Epilogue

The DAO is now working toward 10,000 hectares of Amazon rainforest restored through regenerative cacao agroforestry. It started with a guy named Gary who noticed a gap between what the market was saying and what was actually true — after a silent meditation retreat with Jack Dorsey and a honey purchase that worked without phone reception. Then he couldn't do anything for three years, sitting outside a McDonald's in Boon Lay with Garis Pang, jokingly coining the Do Nothing Society out of sheer exhaustion with half-baked ideas.

The ecosystem generated everything else.
